LoRA: low-rank adaptation

Why

You don’t need to move every weight to change behaviour a lot. The change a narrow task needs is small compared with the model.

What

Freeze the base. Beside each big weight matrix, add two skinny ones, ΔW ≈ B·A, and train only those. A 10,000×10,000 change (100M numbers) becomes 10,000×8 + 8×10,000 (160K numbers).

How

Pick the rank r (8–16 for narrow tasks), the strength alpha (≈ 2×r), and which layers get adapters (attention + MLP). Trains roughly 0.1–2% of the parameters.

Don’t retrain the brain: clip targeted sticky notes onto existing skills. The chef stays intact, so forgetting is limited. The notes are small, and you can swap many sets onto one chef.

Big idea: the adaptation a task needs is low-rank. LoRA freezes the base and trains skinny matrices. The share of trainable parameters is well under 1% on big models; on our tiny 0.5B lab model it's about 2% (about 8.8M parameters), and the saved adapter is about 35 MB. Because B starts at zero, the model behaves exactly like the base at step 0, which the lab uses for a free baseline.
