Why fine-tuning exists: three reasons

1

Reliable behaviour

A prompt is a suggestion to a probabilistic system. At 10,000 replies a day, “usually follows the style” means hundreds of misses. Fine-tuning makes the behaviour the default.

2

A real skill or format gap

A strict schema, a niche citation format, a low-resource language. If the model can’t do it, no prompt will fix it. You have to teach it.

3

Cost and latency at scale

Fine-tune a small model to match a prompted giant on one narrow task: cheaper and faster on every call, forever.

Fine-tuning is for behaviour, skill, style and format. It is a clumsy, expensive way to teach facts, especially facts that change.

Three reasons: reliability of behaviour, genuine skill or format gaps, and cost and latency at scale. The lab hits all three: a strict JSON schema, consistent labels, and a 0.5B model small enough to run on every ticket.
