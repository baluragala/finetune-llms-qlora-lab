QLoRA and the adapter family

Why

Even a frozen base must sit in GPU memory. At 16 bits, big models don’t fit on one modest GPU.

What

Quantize the frozen base to 4-bit (NF4), about 4× less memory, then train LoRA adapters on top in 16-bit. The adapters absorb most of the precision loss.

How

Load with a 4-bit config, freeze, add LoRA, turn on gradient checkpointing. The pragmatic default on a single GPU, and what the lab runs on a free T4.

Adapters in general: small trainable modules inserted into a frozen network. LoRA is the most popular kind. Their value is modularity: one base, many swappable skills.

QLoRA: coach the apprentice against a compressed reference copy of the master chef. Slightly lower fidelity, far less room needed.

QLoRA = quantize the frozen base to 4-bit, then LoRA on top. It democratized fine-tuning: big models on one modest GPU. NF4 is a 4-bit format shaped like the bell curve of real weights, so it loses less than plain int4. Adapters are the general modular family.
